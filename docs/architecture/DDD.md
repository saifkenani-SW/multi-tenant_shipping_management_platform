# Domain-Driven Design (DDD)

## 1. Domain Philosophy

The project follows Domain-Driven Design (DDD).

Business rules are the center of the system.

Technology exists only to support the business.

Every implementation decision must preserve business correctness before technical convenience.

The Domain Layer is the heart of the application.

---

# 2. Ubiquitous Language

The following business terms are considered canonical.

Tenant

Shipping Company operating on the platform.

---

Platform Owner

Administrator of the SaaS platform.

Responsible for managing tenants and subscriptions.

---

Customer

A global user who may interact with multiple tenants.

Customers own a single global identity.

---

Employee

A user employed by a tenant.

Employees perform logistics operations according to assigned permissions.

---

Shipment Request

Commercial request created before logistics operations begin.

---

Quotation

Pricing proposal generated by a shipping company.

---

Customer Shipment

Commercial shipment accepted by both customer and company.

A Customer Shipment groups one or more parcels.

---

Parcel

Smallest physical logistics unit.

Every parcel is tracked individually.

---

Trip

Transportation operation between organization units.

---

Manifest

Collection of parcels transported during one trip.

---

Proof Of Delivery

Evidence proving successful parcel collection.

---

Invoice

Financial record generated for a shipment.

---

Organization Unit

Operational facility.

Examples:

- Branch
- Warehouse
- Hub

---

# 3. Aggregate Roots

The following Aggregate Roots define consistency boundaries.

Platform

Subscription

Organization Unit

Role

Employee

Customer Profile

Shipment Request

Quotation

Customer Shipment

Parcel

Trip

Manifest

Invoice

Support Ticket

---

Every Aggregate Root owns its internal consistency.

External modules must never modify aggregate state directly.

---

# 4. Entities

Entities have identity.

Examples:

Employee

Parcel

Manifest Item

Quotation

Invoice

Vehicle

Support Ticket

Organization Unit

---

# 5. Value Objects

Value Objects have no identity.

Examples include:

Money

Weight

Dimensions

Tracking Number

Phone Number

Email

OTP

Address

Geo Location

Proof Metadata

Business rules should prefer Value Objects whenever identity is unnecessary.

---

# 6. Repository Rules

Repositories belong to Aggregate Roots.

Every Aggregate Root owns one Repository.

Repositories are interfaces defined by the Domain.

Infrastructure provides implementations.

Repositories must never expose ORM models.

Repositories must return Domain objects.

---

# 7. Domain Services

Domain Services exist only when business logic does not naturally belong to a single Aggregate.

Examples:

Pricing Service

Quotation Service

Tracking Service

POD Verification Service

Notification Decision Service

Weight Calculation Service

---

Infrastructure concerns are not Domain Services.

---

# 8. Domain Events

Domain Events describe completed business facts.

Examples:

ShipmentRequestCreated

QuotationGenerated

QuotationApproved

ShipmentCreated

ParcelCreated

TripStarted

TripCompleted

ParcelDelivered

ProofOfDeliveryCompleted

InvoiceGenerated

PaymentCompleted

Events describe the past.

Commands describe the future.

Never confuse them.

---

# 9. Aggregate Boundaries

Aggregates protect consistency.

External modules may reference Aggregate IDs only.

Never hold references to another Aggregate's internal entities.

Communication between Aggregates occurs through:

- IDs
- Domain Events
- Application Services

---

# 10. Aggregate Size

Aggregates should remain small.

Do not load unnecessary relationships.

Do not create giant Aggregate Roots.

Consistency is more important than navigation convenience.

---

# 11. Business Rules

Business rules belong only to the Domain Layer.

Business rules must never appear inside:

- Controllers
- DTOs
- Prisma Models
- Repositories
- Infrastructure

---

# 12. Domain Exceptions

Business rule violations produce Domain Exceptions.

Examples:

ShipmentAlreadyApproved

TripAlreadyStarted

ParcelAlreadyCollected

InvalidStateTransition

DuplicateTrackingNumber

Business exceptions are part of the Domain.

---

# 13. Specifications

Complex business predicates should be implemented using Specifications.

Examples:

EligibleForQuotationSpecification

TripReadyToStartSpecification

ParcelReadyForCollectionSpecification

EmployeeCanApproveShipmentSpecification

Specifications encapsulate reusable business decisions.

---

# 14. Policies

Policies represent configurable business behavior.

Examples:

Pricing Policy

Return Policy

Delivery Verification Policy

Cancellation Policy

Tenant Operational Policy

Policies allow tenant-specific behavior without modifying the Domain Model.

---

# 15. Factories

Factories create Aggregates.

Factories should be used whenever Aggregate creation requires business validation.

Factories prevent invalid object construction.

---

# 16. Domain Integrity

The Domain must always protect:

- Aggregate Invariants.
- State Machines.
- Tenant Isolation.
- Business Consistency.

Infrastructure failures must never corrupt Domain integrity.

---

# 17. Forbidden Practices

The following practices are prohibited.

- Business logic inside Controllers.
- Business logic inside DTOs.
- Business logic inside Prisma models.
- Domain depending on NestJS.
- Domain depending on Prisma.
- Domain depending on HTTP.
- Repository returning ORM entities.
- Cross-module Aggregate mutation.
- Bypassing Aggregate behavior.
- Bypassing Repository interfaces.

Violation of these rules is considered an architectural defect.

---

# 18. DDD Philosophy

The purpose of DDD is not abstraction.

The purpose is protecting business knowledge.

The Domain Model is the most valuable asset of the system.

Everything else exists only to support it.
